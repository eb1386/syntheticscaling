# Literature Review — Teacher size for synthetic training data in small language models

Workstream: LITERATURE REVIEW. Date: 2026-09-10. Brief: `00_brief.md` (project "Synthetic Scaling Laws: Optimal Teacher Model Size for Synthetic Data Generation in Small Language Models").

## 0. Scope, verification procedure, and how to read the tags

* Every paper below was checked online during this session for **title, authors, year, venue/arXiv id** unless tagged otherwise. Verification used web search result snippets plus page fetches from the hosts that were reachable from this sandbox (github.com, microsoft.com). arxiv.org, huggingface.co, aclanthology.org, openreview.net, PMLR, NeurIPS/ICLR/CVPR proceedings, Semantic Scholar, Nature and most mirrors were blocked by the egress proxy, and the web-search budget (200 calls) was exhausted at the end. Consequently:
  * **[VERIFIED]** = title/authors/id/abstract-level claims confirmed from at least one independent online source this session.
  * **[UNVERIFIED-detail]** = the paper is verified but a specific number (model size, token count) is from memory and could not be re-checked.
  * **[UNVERIFIED]** = canonical paper I could not re-check this session at all (I list only well-known ones this way, and only where they are background rather than load-bearing).
* Numbers quoted for prior results are those reported by the authors or by secondary summaries, not re-derived.
* "Threat level" (none / partial / direct) is about the **novelty** of this project's central claim: *for a student of size S, which teacher size T yields the most useful synthetic training data under equal-token control, for pretraining-scale students (25M–1B) in a 5×6 teacher×student grid within one teacher family, with a separate cost analysis.*

Notation used throughout: S = student parameters, T = teacher parameters, D_syn = synthetic tokens (student tokenizer), P(S,T,D) = student metric after continued training on D tokens from teacher T; ΔP_syn(S,T) = P(S,T,D_syn) − P_C0(S); ΔP_real(S) = P_C1(S,D_syn) − P_C0(S). Define the **transfer efficiency relative to real data** η(S,T) = ΔP_syn(S,T)/ΔP_real(S); the **performance-optimal teacher** T*(S) = argmax_T ΔP_syn(S,T) at fixed D_syn; the **cost-optimal teacher** T_$(S) = argmax_T ΔP_syn(S,T,D(T)) with D(T) = B / c(T), where c(T) is measured generation cost per student-token and B a fixed generation budget. Bansal et al.'s compute-matching rule is the special case D(T) ∝ 1/T (FLOPs ∝ T per token).

## 1. Executive summary

1. **The qualitative phenomenon the project targets is already established many times over.** "Bigger teacher ≠ better student" is documented for vision KD (Cho & Hariharan 2019; Mirzadeh et al. 2020), for logit KD of LMs (Zhang et al. 2023/ACL 2025 "law of capacity gap"; Zhong et al. 2024; Busbridge et al. 2025; Lu & Liu 2026), for instruction-tuning data generators (Xu et al. 2024/NAACL 2025), for reasoning data (Bansal et al. 2024; Li et al. 2025), and for pretraining rephrasers (Pieler et al. 2024; BeyondWeb 2025; Kang et al. 2025 EMNLP; Niklaus et al. 2026). The project must not present H1–H3 as open questions in general; it can only present them as *untested in its specific regime*.
2. **The specific regime is not covered by any single prior work**: sequence-level synthetic data (not logits), a controlled S×T grid with S ∈ [25M, 1B] and T ∈ [1.5B, 72B] from one instruct family, equal student-tokenizer tokens as the primary control, a shared real-data base with a matched-real-tokens control (C1), and generation cost measured rather than FLOP-estimated. The closest works are Kang et al. 2025 (generator size × student size for rephrased/textbook pretraining data, 100M–3B students, from-scratch mixtures), Niklaus et al. 2026 (generator-size sweep for rephrasing; result: no gain beyond 1B), Xu et al. 2024 (20 generators × 5 ≈7–9B students, instruction tuning), and Busbridge et al. 2025 (logit KD; capacity-gap scaling law).
3. **Prior evidence predicts early saturation.** Four independent pretraining-data studies find generator size saturating between ~1B and ~8B (Niklaus 2026: >1B no gain; BeyondWeb: 1B→3B gain, saturates by 8B; Kang 2025: >~8B not better; SmolLM/Cosmopedia: 70B-class generators not better than Mixtral-8x7B), and Pieler 2024 finds family matters more than size. Within one family, Li et al. 2026 ("Rethinking OPD") report that 1.5B and 7B same-family teachers are "distributionally indistinguishable from the student's perspective". The prior expectation is therefore that H2 holds with a small T_sat, that H3 may or may not appear, and that H4 may be unfalsifiable if the T-axis is flat above ~3B. The project must be powered to detect small effects and must include a cross-family teacher control.
4. **H4 has a direct precedent** in the logit-KD channel: Zhang et al. (ACL 2025) report that "the optimal teacher consistently scales linearly with the student scale" for <3B students, and Busbridge et al. express the optimum in teacher-loss space. Any T*(S) fit here must be framed as *testing whether the logit-KD result transfers to sequence-level synthetic data*, not as discovering the relation.
5. **H5 has a direct precedent** in Bansal et al. 2024 (compute-matched sampling: weaker+cheaper wins) and a partial one in Busbridge et al. (compute allocation including teacher inference). The novelty available is the *joint* presentation of equal-token and iso-cost views across a full grid with measured cost, and the identification of where T* and T_$ diverge as a function of S.
6. **Title**: "Synthetic Scaling Laws" is not defensible (Qin et al. 2025 and Kang et al. 2025 already own "scaling laws of synthetic data", Busbridge owns "distillation scaling laws", and 30 cells in one family cannot establish a law). Three alternatives are proposed in §8.

## 2. Conceptual map: three transfer channels and three training regimes

| Channel | What the student sees | Representative work | Where this project sits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Logit / distribution KD | Teacher token distributions on (usually real) text | Hinton 2015; Busbridge 2025; Zhang 2025; Lu & Liu 2026; Peng 2025; He 2026 | Not this project (tokenizer mismatch makes it impossible anyway) |
| Sequence-level / off-policy synthetic data (SeqKD) | Teacher-generated text, trained with plain cross-entropy | Kim & Rush 2016; TinyStories; phi; Cosmopedia; WRAP; Bansal 2024; Xu 2024; Kang 2025; Niklaus 2026 | **This project** |
| On-policy distillation (OPD) | Teacher scores on student's own samples | Agarwal 2024 GKD; MiniLLM; Qwen3; Thinking Machines 2025; Li 2026; Armandpour 2026 | Not this project; relevant for teacher-choice findings |

| Regime | Student state | Representative work | This project |
|---|---|---|---|
| From-scratch pretraining | Random init | TinyStories, phi-1, Cosmopedia/SmolLM, WRAP, Kang 2025, Niklaus 2026, Busbridge 2025 | Rejected as primary design (Option A/C in brief) |
| Mid-training / continued pretraining | Pretrained base, more tokens | EntiGraph (Yang 2024), Nemotron-CC/Nemotron-H stages, He 2026, Tao 2026 | **This project** (Option B) |
| Post-training / SFT | Instruction tuning | Xu 2024, Bansal 2024, Li 2025, GRACE, PerSyn, SCAS | Related but not identical; benchmark suite (P6) is closer to pretraining-style evaluation |

The project's mechanism is therefore best described as **sequence-level distillation via synthetic continued pretraining**, with teacher size as the manipulated variable. This framing matters for citations and for what the results can generalise to.

## 5. Do Busbridge 2025, Bansal 2024 and Xu 2024 already answer the core question?

**Short answer**: each answers a *neighbouring* question; none answers the project's question; together they make the project's *qualitative* conclusions predictable and therefore force the project to be about quantities, controls and regime, not about the existence of an optimum.

| Dimension | Busbridge et al. 2025 | Bansal et al. 2024 | Xu et al. 2024 | This project |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transfer channel | Token-level logit KD (KL to teacher distribution on real text) | Sequence-level synthetic solutions (SFT on teacher samples, answer-filtered) | Sequence-level synthetic responses (SFT) | Sequence-level synthetic text, no answer filtering beyond fixed rules |

Points of precision:

1. *Busbridge*: their capacity-gap result is expressed as a function of teacher cross-entropy and student compute, not raw parameter count; they explicitly say the gap is "a gap in learning capacity ... and not only about their relative sizes, which is a special case". If the project fits T*(S) in parameter space only, it will be a coarser version of their analysis. Recommendation: also fit in teacher-loss space (teacher NLL on the prompt-conditioned real reference data) and in "teacher–student NLL gap" space; the latter connects to Xu's CAR and to GRACE.
2. *Bansal*: the headline "weaker is better" is conditional on the weaker model generating ~3× more samples at equal FLOPs. At equal number of samples their data show the stronger model is often better or comparable [UNVERIFIED-detail from memory of their appendix]. The project's equal-token primary control therefore tests a *different* claim (data quality per token vs teacher size); the iso-cost secondary analysis reproduces Bansal's framing. Both must be reported; reporting only equal-token invites the objection that the relevant practical question is iso-cost.
3. *Xu*: closest in spirit (teacher choice is student-dependent; "compatibility" measured by student NLL of teacher responses). The project extends the student axis downward by 1–2 orders of magnitude and removes family confounds, but should adopt CAR-style compatibility as a covariate. Xu's students are all in the same size class, so it says nothing about H4.

## 7. Frank novelty assessment

**Genuinely unaddressed (as far as this review could verify):**
1. A controlled teacher-size × student-size grid for *sequence-level* synthetic data at *pretraining-scale student sizes (25M–1B)* with a single post-trained teacher family, equal student-tokenizer tokens, and a matched-real-token control. Kang 2025 comes closest but with from-scratch mixtures, rephrasing/textbook data, and (apparently) no per-student optimum analysis; Niklaus 2026 sweeps generator size but for rephrasing and (apparently) one student size; Xu 2024 has the design but at 7–9B and across families.
2. Whether the logit-KD result of Zhang 2025 (linear T*(S)) and Busbridge 2025 (optimum in teacher-loss space) *transfers* to the sequence-level channel where the student never sees teacher probabilities. This is a real question because SeqKD discards the "dark knowledge" that the capacity-gap literature usually invokes.
3. The divergence between T*(S) and T_$(S) as a function of S with *measured* generation cost on workstation hardware (rather than analytic FLOPs), including the observation that inference cost per token is not linear in T on a fixed GPU (batching, KV-cache limits, FP8 vs BF16).
4. A decomposition of the teacher effect into mediators (teacher NLL on reference data; student-base NLL on teacher data = compatibility/CAR; output entropy and diversity; length) to test whether "size" has any effect once compatibility is controlled — a mediation analysis that none of the ten closest works performs.

**Incremental (already shown in a neighbouring regime; the project can confirm/extend but not claim discovery):**
* "Larger teachers are not always better" (10+ papers).
* "Returns saturate at mid-size generators" (BeyondWeb, Niklaus, Kang, SmolLM).
* "Cheaper teacher can be compute-optimal" (Bansal; Niklaus).
* "The best teacher depends on the student" (Xu; GRACE; SCAS; PerSyn; Armandpour; Li 2025).
* "Synthetic continued pretraining scales with synthetic tokens" (EntiGraph; Qin).
* "Family/prompt/source matter more than generator size" (Pieler; Niklaus; SmolLM) — the project's single-family design deliberately holds these fixed, which strengthens internal validity but prevents any claim about their relative importance.

**Claims the project must NOT make:**
* "We propose synthetic scaling laws" / "distillation scaling laws" / "law of capacity gap" — all taken (Qin 2025; Kang 2025; Busbridge 2025; Zhang 2025).
* "First to show that a smaller teacher can produce a better student" (false since 2019).
* "T* ∝ S^α" as a law; with 5 student sizes and 6 teacher sizes in one family, at most a *descriptive* relation with confidence bands, and only if the optimum is interior and identifiable.
* Any claim about generality across teacher families, prompt styles, or data types (QA vs rephrasing vs CoT) unless a cross-family teacher control is added.
* Any claim that separates "teacher size" from "teacher post-training recipe" while using instruct models whose small sizes may themselves be distilled (documented for Qwen3, Gemma 2, Llama 3.2, Ministral 3; status for Qwen2.5 [UNVERIFIED]).
* Any claim about model collapse (single-generation data with retained real base is not the collapse setting; Gerstgrasser 2024; Schaeffer 2025).
* That equal-token is *the* correct control; Bansal's iso-cost framing is the practitioner's question and must be co-reported.

## 9. Implications for the experimental design (literature-driven)

1. **Power**: within-family teacher effects may be small (Li 2026; Pieler 2024; Niklaus 2026). Pilot must estimate seed variance at 25M/100M and compute the minimum detectable effect; 3 seeds may not resolve a 0.5-point benchmark difference. Continuous NLL on held-out human QA/instruction text (P6) is the better-powered primary metric; Gadre 2024 provides the NLL→downstream mapping.
2. **Cross-family control**: add at least one non-Qwen teacher at a matched size (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct or Gemma-3-12B-it) to bound the "family > size" effect (Pieler 2024). Without it, a flat T-axis is uninterpretable.
3. **Mediators to log per teacher**: mean/entropy of output length; per-token entropy under the fixed decoding; diversity (Chen 2024 cluster-agent or simpler n-gram/embedding measures); judge-rated correctness/false-positive rate (Bansal 2024); teacher NLL on human reference answers; student-base NLL on each teacher's data (CAR, Xu 2024). Then test whether T explains ΔP beyond these mediators.
4. **Iso-cost secondary analysis** (Bansal): use the nested D-sweep (P5) to interpolate P(S,T,D) and evaluate at D(T) = B/c(T) with measured c(T). Report T*(S) and T_$(S) side by side.
5. **Mixing**: Kang 2025 finds pure synthetic slower than natural and ~30% synthetic optimal; He 2026 finds mid-training KD can hurt factual recall. Add an ablation where the continued phase mixes synthetic with real tokens (e.g., 1:2) and split metrics into knowledge-heavy (SciQ, ARC, OBQA) vs reasoning/commonsense (PIQA, HellaSwag, Winogrande) plus NLL.
6. **Baseline reporting** (Honda 2026): every cell relative to C0 *and* C1; a teacher can be "best" while still below C1.
7. **Decoding**: identical temperature/top-p ≠ identical effective sampling entropy across teacher sizes (Dohmatob 2024 tails argument). Either report entropy or add a temperature-calibrated ablation for one student size.
8. **Teacher family**: Qwen2.5-Instruct is a reasonable choice but check the Qwen2.5 report for whether small instruct models were trained on outputs of larger ones [UNVERIFIED]. Using *base* (non-instruct) teachers with few-shot prompting for one student size would test whether the effect is about size or about post-training.
9. **Floors**: MobileLLM-125M averages 46.3% on an 8-task suite (Liu 2024); at 25M most multiple-choice tasks will be within noise of chance; rely on NLL and on LAMBADA/SciQ/ARC-Easy/PIQA, which have the highest signal at small scale in Pythia-class models.
10. **Framing**: describe the method as sequence-level distillation via synthetic continued pretraining (Kim & Rush 2016; Yang 2024), cite Busbridge/Zhang/Lu & Liu as the logit-channel precedents, and Kang/Niklaus/BeyondWeb/Pieler/SmolLM as the generator-size precedents.

## Open issues and disagreements with the brief

1. **P9 list is incomplete in ways that matter.** The brief omits the works that most directly threaten H1/H2 in the *pretraining-data* channel: Kang et al. 2025 (EMNLP; generator size × student size), Niklaus et al. 2026 (generator >1B no gain; 30× cheaper), BeyondWeb 2025 (1B→3B→8B saturation), Pieler et al. 2024 (family > size), the SmolLM/Cosmopedia generator comparison, and, in the logit channel, Zhang et al. ACL 2025 (linear T*(S)) and Lu & Liu 2026. It also omits the 2025–26 teacher-selection literature (GRACE, PerSyn, SCAS, Armandpour 2026, Li 2026 "Rethinking OPD"). Related-work and novelty statements must be rewritten around these.
2. **Title/framing (P8)**: agree that "Synthetic Scaling Laws" is not defensible; additionally "Optimal Teacher Model Size" in the subtitle presupposes an interior optimum that four prior studies suggest may not exist above ~3B. Prefer T1/T3 above.
3. **H4 as stated is at risk of being unfalsifiable.** If the T-axis is flat above the smallest teachers, "T* ∝ S^α" cannot be estimated. Pre-register an identifiability criterion and a null model (flat above T_sat, T_sat independent of S), and treat the linear law of Zhang 2025 as the alternative hypothesis to test rather than assuming a power law.
4. **P2 equal-token as primary control**: defensible for the *scientific* question (data quality per token), but the literature's practitioner question is iso-cost (Bansal). Disagreement: iso-cost should be a co-primary analysis, not "separate"; the nested D-sweep makes it cheap.
5. **P1 pure-synthetic continued phase** conflicts with Kang 2025 (pure rephrased synthetic ≤ natural; ~30% mix optimal) and He 2026 (mid-training KD harms factual recall). Recommend a mixed-continuation arm (synthetic:real) at least in the pilot, and knowledge-vs-reasoning metric split.
6. **P3 teacher family**: Qwen2.5-Instruct small models may be trained on larger-model outputs [UNVERIFIED]; if so, "teacher size" is partly "distillation depth". Add a base-model-teacher ablation and one cross-family teacher; consider reporting teacher NLL on reference data as the x-axis (Busbridge's framing) alongside parameter count.
7. **P6 benchmarks**: at 25M–100M most listed tasks are at floor (MobileLLM-125M avg 46%). Primary metric should be NLL on held-out human QA/instruction text plus the subset of tasks above floor; keep the full suite for 250M–1B.
8. **P7 seeds**: prior within-family effects are small; 3 seeds at 25M may be underpowered. Recommend a variance pilot before fixing the seed tier.
9. **Verification limits of this review**: arXiv, HF, ACL Anthology, OpenReview, PMLR and proceedings sites were blocked and the search budget was exhausted; ~8 details are tagged [UNVERIFIED-detail] (Busbridge size/token grid; Xu base-model list; Bansal equal-sample result; WRAP student sizes; Chen 2024 student sizes; whether Kang 2025 reports a per-student optimum; whether Niklaus 2026 varies student size; SynPro and Tao 2026 arXiv ids). A follow-up with direct access should close these before the related-work section is finalised.
