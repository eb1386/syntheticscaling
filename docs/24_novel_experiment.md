# 24. The Experiment (C4): what governs synthetic-data scaling for small LMs

This document defines the study the code now runs by default (`c4` profile). It reframes the
project from "which teacher gives the best score" into a program whose questions have not, to
our knowledge, been cleanly answered together. Honesty first: this design targets novel
questions and is built to a pre-registered, controlled standard, but novelty is proven by
results, not by ambition, and main-track acceptance also depends on the writeup. Every test
below is constructed so that a clean negative is a real, reportable finding.

## 24.1 Research question

We train a family of small students (25M to 1B) from matched base checkpoints on synthetic
data generated by teachers spanning two families and 0.5B to 72B parameters, measure held-out
loss throughout training to estimate scaling curves, and ask:

**Is synthetic-data scaling for small LMs governed by teacher size, teacher family, or by
measurable, student-referenced properties of the data itself?**

## 24.2 The five targets (each falsifiable, each with a clean negative)

- **N1 rate vs level.** Does the generator change the data-scaling exponent alpha in
  L(D)=E+A·D^(-alpha), or only the asymptote E? Prior work reports levels; the rate-vs-level
  decomposition at pretraining scale is not established. Test: per-cell fits, then decompose
  variance of alpha vs E explained by the teacher.
- **N2 screening (centerpiece).** Is teacher identity (size and family) screened off by a
  measured, student-referenced data-quality index q? We predict a cell's scaling parameters
  from (log size + family), from q alone, and from both, by leave-one-teacher-out cross
  validation. If q predicts as well as identity and identity adds nothing beyond q, then
  scaling is governed by measurable data properties, not by which model wrote the data. This
  is the result we believe has not been shown.
- **N3 predictive law.** Fit L(S, T, D) on a subset of cells and predict held-out cells out of
  sample. Out-of-sample validation of a synthetic-data scaling law is rare; it turns "we
  measured curves" into "we found a law that transfers."
- **N4 compute-optimal allocation.** At a fixed total FLOP budget split between teacher
  generation (~2T per token) and student training (~6N per token), which teacher size
  minimises student loss, and how does the optimum move with student size? A practical frontier
  not mapped at this granularity.
- **N5 cross-family multiplier invariance.** Is the synthetic-data multiplier D_real/D_syn a
  function of q alone, invariant to family? A single-variable law would be a strong, portable
  result.

## 24.3 The data-quality index q (the instrument that makes N2 possible)

For each teacher T and student S we measure three properties of the teacher's data and combine
their z-scores into a scalar q (higher = better data); the regression also uses the raw
components. Implemented in `synscale/analysis/data_properties.py`.

- **Learnability** (student-referenced, the key term): mean per-token NLL of the teacher's
  responses under the student's own base checkpoint theta*(S). Low = compatible with what the
  student can already model. This is the operational form of the capacity-gap / compatibility
  idea (Xu 2024 CAR; Busbridge 2025), measured directly per student.
- **Diversity**: distinct-n and self-repetition on responses.
- **Correctness**: fraction correct on the verifiable subset, from generation gold labels.

Because learnability is measured against each student, q is student-specific, which is exactly
what a capacity-dependent theory predicts.

## 24.4 Design (C4 profile)

| Axis | Values |
|---|---|
| Students S | 25M, 50M, 100M, 250M, 500M, 1B (one LLaMA-style family, shared 16k BPE) |
| Teacher family A | Qwen2.5-Instruct 0.5B, 1.5B, 3B, 7B, 14B, 32B, 72B (all students) |
| Teacher family B | Llama-3.x 3B, 8B, 70B, at 100M and 1B only (cross-family screen, cost-efficient) |
| Data D | ~20 points per cell from 25M to 400M, read from the training curve |
| Controls | C1 matched real tokens, C1b human Q&A, C0 base |
| Seeds | 3 per cell (base seeds: 3 at 25M, 1 elsewhere) |

Serving precision is uniform FP8 for every teacher (quantized on load); 70B and 72B run
tensor-parallel over two cards. Uniform precision keeps precision from becoming a
size-correlated confound; a BF16-vs-FP8 bridge cell at one mid teacher measures the residual.
Total: 188 training runs. Everything else (matched checkpoints, WSD branching, equal-token
generation, teacher-independent evaluation, seed-level uncertainty) is inherited from the
locked methodology (`docs/00`) and the scaling method (`docs/22`).

## 24.5 Why this is more novel than the earlier framing

The earlier framing ("which teacher size is best") is incremental: the direction is known. The
shift to (a) rate-vs-level, (b) screening teacher identity with a measured data index, (c) an
out-of-sample-validated law, and (d) a two-family test of invariance moves the contribution
from "confirm a known direction" to "identify the variable that actually governs the scaling,
and show model identity is or is not screened off by it." That is a mechanism claim, and
mechanism claims backed by controlled measurement are what main-track venues reward.

## 24.6 What it still cannot claim

Generality beyond these two families and this prompt protocol; that q is causal rather than
predictive (mediation is correlational with 10 teachers; the within-teacher difficulty-quartile
ablation in `docs/15` is the only causal lever); anything about teachers above 72B or students
above 1B. These belong in Limitations, stated plainly.
